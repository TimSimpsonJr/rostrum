# Defaults for a how-to video

What a how-to gets unless the narration or the user's instructions call for something else. A showcase, an
explainer or any other kind of video takes its shape from its own narration and brief, not from this page.

## The script

- **Shape:** a title that says what the viewer will be able to do, as a short imperative ("Find your user API
  key"); one line per step; a closing line, "And that's how you <the title, as a clause>.", with no recap.
- **Second person, present tense, plain:** "Open your user menu", "You'll see your name".
- **Controls are named as the interface labels them,** capitals included ("select View API Key"). A control with no
  label is named by what it is and where ("your user menu in the top right corner").
- **Don't explain the domain beyond what the recorded narration does.**
- **Where before what** when the camera has to travel ("At the bottom of the page, select ..."); the first line
  says where the thing is.
- **One sentence per line; only what the walkthrough showed.**
- **Length:** a voice reads about 2.5 words a second, so 30 to 60 seconds is about 75 to 140 words. The first
  tutorial is 65 words in 7 lines and runs 30 seconds.
- **For the ear:** short words, no brackets, symbols or abbreviations the voice would stumble on.

## The film

- **It opens on the title alone and closes on the logo,** the closing line playing over the logo's reveal (with no
  brand, the title as text). No recap frames.
- **The whole screen first, then in.** A later screen opens on its widest framing too.
- **One continuous camera per screen:** it goes in and does not pull back.
- **The cursor is the pointer.** One cursor: it enters once, travels on eased paths worked out from its targets
  (not the recorded mouse trail), clicks, and leaves once. It lands on a blank part of what it clicks and rests
  beside what it only points at, clear of the text.
- **Chalk marks** (circles, arrows, underlines) on request.
